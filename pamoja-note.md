# Note for Pamoja

**Subject:** Kenya food prices (maize, 2006–2019) — what the WFP data says for farmers and county officers

**Data source:** WFP Kenya – Food Prices on the Humanitarian Data Exchange (HDX), licence **CC BY-IGO 3.0**, downloaded 24 October 2026. 2,742 rows: 721 wholesale (90-kg bags, 5 markets) and 2,021 retail (1 kg, 9 markets), Jan 2006 – Dec 2019.


## Advice for farmers

1. **Store maize through the dry season and sell into the June peak.** Wholesale maize averaged **KES 2,329.52 per 90-kg bag in January** but **KES 2,839.47 in June** — about 22% higher. In 73.33% of markets (44 of 60) June was above January, with a median gain of **KES 356 per bag**. The lean-season storage play pays more often than it loses.

2. **Time sales to the calendar, not the harvest.** January is the cheapest month on average for wholesale maize; June is the dearest. Hold grain and sell into the June peak, or sell early to capture the lowest official-market prices.

3. **Brace for a volatile market.** The single biggest yearly jump was **2011, +1,246.27 KES (+70.3%)**, and the single biggest fall was **2018, −1,335.29 KES (−35.7%)**. A season can more than double wholesale prices, so never bank on one good year: stagger sales and hold a reserve through the lean months.

## Advice for county officers

1. **Focus county interventions on Lodwar and Mandera, where retail maize premiums persist.** They are the only two markets that ranked in the top three for retail maize every year between 2015 and 2019. Lodwar was the most expensive market on average at **KES 75.85 per kg**, roughly **2× Kitui (36.19/kg)**. Feeder roads, grain-aggregation, or local distribution investment in these towns reaches the households paying the most.
